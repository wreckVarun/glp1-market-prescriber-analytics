# Memo: Where Mounjaro's sales force should focus

**To:** Mounjaro (tirzepatide) US brand team, Eli Lilly
**From:** Commercial analytics
**Data:** CMS Medicare Part D Prescribers by Provider and Drug, 2020 to 2024 (latest year CMS has released). 1.31M prescriber-drug-year rows.

## Bottom line
The Medicare GLP-1 market is still growing fast (+39% claims in 2024), and Mounjaro is taking most of the new volume. Two groups hold Mounjaro's biggest opportunity: high-volume primary care prescribers who still write Ozempic by default, and Lilly's own Trulicity writers, who are leaving the brand. California is the largest under-penetrated state. Concentrate calls on about 40,000 high-value prescribers, use a lighter-touch program for about 37,000 emerging ones, and cover everyone else through non-personal channels.

## What the data shows
- **Market size:** 19.6M GLP-1 Part D claims and $24.6B gross drug cost in 2024, 4.1x the 2020 level. The 2025 base case is 27.5M claims (range 25.6M to 29.5M) and about $35B gross.
- **Share:** Ozempic 51%, Mounjaro 24% (up from 11% in 2023), Trulicity 19%. Lilly's total share fell from 45% to 43% because Trulicity claims dropped 21% (4.85M to 3.82M) while Mounjaro tripled.
- **Concentration:** the top 20% of prescribers (40,232 writers with 138+ GLP-1 claims) write 59% of all claims. Mounjaro holds 27% of their volume.
- **Who prescribes:** primary care drives the volume. Family practice, internal medicine, nurse practitioners and physician assistants write 82% of claims. Endocrinologists write only 13%, but at 424 claims each they write 4 to 5 times as much as a family practice prescriber or nurse practitioner.

## Where to focus
1. **Convert high-value Ozempic loyalists (largest prize).** 27,407 of the 40,232 high-value prescribers write mostly Ozempic. Within the high-value group, 3,957 prescribers show no visible Mounjaro at all, and together they write 783K GLP-1 claims. Make these prescribers the core call list, with full rep frequency.
2. **Keep Trulicity patients inside Lilly.** Trulicity lost about 1M claims in 2024. The 8,881 high-value and emerging prescribers whose top brand is Trulicity need a dedicated switch-to-Mounjaro message before they move those patients to Ozempic.
3. **Fix the California gap.** California is the second-largest state (8.4% of claims, +42% growth), but Mounjaro's share there is only 15%, against 24% nationally. Reaching the national share would add about 140K claims. Pennsylvania (19%), Illinois (21%) and New York (21%) show smaller versions of the same gap. Add reps there before adding them in Texas, Florida or Georgia, where Mounjaro already holds 28 to 30%.
4. **Build the emerging pipeline.** 37,422 mid-volume prescribers are growing faster than the market. 12,619 of them write no visible Mounjaro, so they are the best place to win first prescriptions. Nurse practitioners are a large share of this group, and Mounjaro already over-indexes with them (26% share).
5. **Use endocrinologists for influence, not reach.** At 4,518 high-value endocrinologists, this is a small list worth calling on for advocacy and peer education, but it cannot carry volume on its own.

## Caveats
- **Medicare only:** commercial patients and most obesity use (Wegovy and Zepbound for weight loss) are excluded, because Part D cannot cover weight-loss-only drugs.
- **Data lag:** CMS releases data about 18 to 24 months late, so the 2025 forecast tests the method rather than predicting the future.
- **Suppression:** rows under 11 claims are hidden. "No visible Mounjaro" means fewer than 11 claims, and totals are slightly understated.
- **Gross cost:** dollar figures are before rebates and are not revenue. Price is held flat; Medicare's negotiated semaglutide price starts in 2027.
- **Short history:** Mounjaro has only 2.5 years of data. Its forecast assumes it adds as many claims in 2025 as it added in 2024, a judgement call rather than a fitted trend.
